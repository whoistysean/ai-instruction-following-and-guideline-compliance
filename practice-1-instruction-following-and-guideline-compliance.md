Exercise 1: Basic Sentiment Classification

Annotation Guidelines:

* Positive: The customer expresses satisfaction.
* Negative: The customer expresses dissatisfaction.
* Neutral: The customer expresses no clear positive or negative opinion.
* Always evaluate the entire review.

Evaluation Results

The delivery was late, and I’m disappointed.	Negative

Exercise 2: Handling Ambiguous Instructions

Customer Review:

“The laptop is expensive, but I guess it’s worth the money.”

Evaluation:

Assigned Label: Positive.

Reasoning:

The phrase “it’s worth the money” communicates approval despite the high price.

The word “expensive” alone does not establish dissatisfaction.

Key Lesson:

Annotators should interpret the overall meaning of a statement instead of relying on isolated keywords.

Skills Demonstrated:

* Contextual interpretation.
* Handling ambiguous sentiment.
* Avoiding keyword-based assumptions.

Exercise 3: Annotation Quality Assurance

An annotator submitted the following labels:

Customer Review	Original Label	Correct Label
The food was delicious.	Positive	Positive
The delivery took five days longer than expected.	Positive	Negative
The phone is okay, nothing special.	Negative	Neutral
I absolutely love this service.	Positive	Positive
The package arrived on Monday.	Neutral	Neutral

Identified Errors

Error 1: Incorrect Positive Label

Review: “The delivery took five days longer than expected.”

The original annotator overlooked the customer’s dissatisfaction with the delivery delay.

Correction: Negative.

Error 2: Incorrect Negative Label

Review: “The phone is okay, nothing special.”

The original annotator interpreted the absence of strong praise as dissatisfaction.

Correction: Neutral.

Quality Assurance Findings

Both errors demonstrate the importance of:

* Reading the entire review.
* Applying the annotation guidelines consistently.
* Avoiding assumptions about customer emotions.
* Distinguishing neutral feedback from negative feedback.

Skills Demonstrated:

* Detecting annotation errors.
* Correcting inconsistent labels.
* Explaining quality assurance decisions.
* Maintaining annotation accuracy.

⸻

Key Lessons

1. Follow the provided annotation guidelines rather than personal opinions.
2. Evaluate the entire statement before assigning a label.
3. Do not classify sentiment using isolated keywords.
4. Expensive does not automatically mean negative.
5. Lack of strong praise does not automatically mean dissatisfaction.
6. Quality assurance helps identify and correct inconsistent annotations.
